# Frontend Design Guide: Key Takeaways

This resource provides strategic direction for creating distinctive, non-templated web design. Here are the core principles:

**Ground Design in Subject Matter**
The guide emphasizes starting by identifying "one concrete subject, its audience, and the page's single job." Design choices should emerge from the actual world of what's being designed, using its "materials, instruments, artifacts, and vernacular."

**Design Principles Overview**
- **Hero as Thesis**: Open with "the most characteristic thing in the subject's world" rather than defaulting to generic patterns
- **Typography as Personality**: Pair typefaces deliberately for each project, avoiding stock combinations
- **Structure Encodes Meaning**: Use structural devices only when they genuinely serve the content (numbered sequences make sense for actual processes, not decoration)
- **Intentional Motion**: Animation should serve the subject; scattered effects can feel AI-generated
- **Match Complexity to Vision**: "Elegance is executing the chosen vision well"

**Process: Two-Pass Approach**
First, develop a compact design plan with color, typography, layout, and signature elements. Then critique against the brief to avoid generic defaults before building code.

**Restraint Strategy**
"Spend your boldness in one place" with a signature element, keeping surroundings quiet and disciplined. Cut decoration that doesn't serve the brief.

**Writing as Design Material**
Copy should help users understand and navigate; it's not decoration. Use active voice, specificity over cleverness, and conversational tone matched to audience.
